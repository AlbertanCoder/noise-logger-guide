Build a Noise Logger

A small box that listens all day and keeps an automatic, timestamped record of noise: when it happened, how long, how loud and what kind of sound it was. It never keeps audio when it hears talking, and its log shows if anything was changed later.

How it works — and what it can't do

A microphone near the ceiling measures loudness 10 times a second. Anything louder than the room's normal quiet, for half a second or with a sharp thump, becomes one event. Each event gets a time (Alberta time), length, loudness, number of thumps and up to 3 sound types from YAMNet, Google's free sound-recognition model.

  • Talking is never recorded. If a voice is heard, no audio is kept, only the numbers.
  • Changes show. Each log entry carries a fingerprint of the one before it; editing or deleting one breaks the chain.
  • It is not a sound level meter. Loudness is measured relative to its own microphone, not in dB(A), so it is never compared with a bylaw's decibel limits.
  • Only a bylaw officer can confirm a violation. The log is a record of when and how often noise happened.
