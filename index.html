What this is

A box that listens all day and writes down when noise happens, how long, how loud and what kind of sound, so there is an honest dated record to show someone.

On day one it should

  • Log every event: date, time, length, how loud the mic heard it, and likely sound types.
  • Count the night ones (10 pm to 7 am), make a report, and open on your phone.
  • Let you tap Mine, Theirs or Not sure on an event.

The log software passes its own tests on a laptop (92 on 2026-11-22). It has not run on a Pi or with this mic yet.

Fixed in the software on 2026-11-22, tested with made-up sound only
  • Faint short thumps: it now also listens to the thump band on its own (40 to 250 Hz). 100 of 100 made-up thumps were logged; before, 51.
  • Long, steady noise is written in 30-second pieces while it happens, and the thumps inside it are counted.
  • A steady low rumble is no longer counted as thumps. A thump less than about 10 dB above a rumble may not be counted.
  • If the mic is unplugged or silent, the logger stops with an error, and the report shows the hours it was not listening.
  • Stopping or restarting the logger saves the noise in progress. A power cut can still lose the last 31 seconds.
  • Event times are read from the clock as the sound arrives.
  • Each entry stores what decibel estimates will need. The estimates themselves come later.

None of this has heard a real ceiling yet. Step 3 is the first real test.

It does not

  • Act as a legal sound meter. Decibels will be estimates, and only a bylaw officer can confirm a violation.
  • Know whose noise it is. That waits until a learning test passes; until then the report says the source is not decided.
  • Keep conversations. Saving audio is off. The log does note that talking was heard, and when; that note can be wrong (plain hiss set it off in a test), so it never proves that someone spoke. In a test the speech check missed muffled voices under knocks, so thump clips stay off until that is fixed and tested.
  • Play any sound. There is no speaker.

A microphone that listens all day can fall under privacy and recording law. Check your own; nothing here is legal advice.

Later, in this order

  1. Decibel estimates from the mic's calibration file.
  2. Short thump clips, never when talking is heard.
  3. A small sound model that learns whose noise it is from your taps. It counts only if it passes its test.

It could also be used for

  • A portfolio piece: this guide, the model and its checks.
  • A sleep record: what woke you and when.
  • Loud noise from next door.

None of the "later" or "also" items is built, except the tap buttons.

Why this mic

What the noise is

Heavy footsteps through a wood-joist floor are mostly below 100 Hz, a deep thump (National Research Council Canada ↗). In one study the footsteps reached 30 to 55 dB in the room below (University of Liverpool ↗). The first source also says most of the energy that reaches the room below is under 250 Hz.

What the mic must do

Hear down to about 20 Hz, from any direction, with little hiss of its own, and have a known loudness so decibel estimates are possible later.

Why the UMIK-1

  • 20 Hz to 20 kHz, within 1 dB once its own calibration file is used (maker's page).
  • Its hiss is about 30 dB(A) by outside reports. The maker gives its noise only in another unit (-74 dBFS at top gain). By a rough estimate that is below what ears can detect up to about 80 Hz, and above it from about 100 Hz.
  • So: clear stomps and thumps, likely yes. Very faint knocks in a silent room, maybe not. Nobody has tested it on this ceiling yet; step 3 does that.
The other four that were looked at
  • miniDSP UMIK-2, $306.60 (Solen). Quieter and calibrated, but the whole build goes over $500 and the shop's policy only takes back unused items.
  • Rode NT1 Signature + Behringer UMC22, $284 (Amazon). The quietest (4 dBA), but not calibrated and needs the extra box.
  • Rode NT-USB+, $249 (Amazon). One cable, but not calibrated, and Linux is not on its list.
  • Clippy EM272, from £39.84 (UK). Very quiet (about 14 dBA), but not calibrated and needs a USB adapter that wasn't priced.

Prices read 2026-11-22. The same UMIK-1 is $142.80 at Solen, whose policy takes back only unused items, with at least a 10% charge and only with approval first. Amazon's free return also asks for new and unused condition, but needs no approval and has no fixed charge.
